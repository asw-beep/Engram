| hybrid_rerank | 0.890 | 0.973 | 0.824 | 0.872 |

- `dense`: BGE-M3 vectors in Qdrant
- `hybrid`: dense and BM25 (OpenSearch), fused with RRF
- `dense_rerank`: dense, then BGE-reranker-v2-m3
- `hybrid_rerank`: hybrid, then BGE-reranker-v2-m3

A chunk is relevant when it comes from a gold document and its heading path starts with a gold section's. Recall@K counts a question only when every gold section has a chunk in the top K, so a `multi_section` question needs both. NDCG is graded: the first chunk from each gold section earns 2 and the first chunk from elsewhere in a gold document earns 1, so a ranking holding only the gold sections scores below 1 when the rest of a gold document could also have been found.

## Evidence gate (test split)

| Configuration | Cutoff | Cutoff from | Answerable passed | Unanswerable refused | Balanced accuracy |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| dense | 0.500 | configured | 1.000 | 0.500 | 0.750 |
| hybrid | – | not applicable | – | – | – |
| dense_rerank | 0.740 | calibrated on dev | 0.986 | 1.000 | 0.993 |
| hybrid_rerank | 0.740 | calibrated on dev | 0.986 | 1.000 | 0.993 |

A question passes when its highest passage score is at or above the cutoff, the rule `answer_question` applies. This is the score gate only: the API also refuses when every passage above the cutoff is too large for the context budget, which the harness doesn't model. Balanced accuracy is the mean of the two shares. A `configured` cutoff is taken from the settings as given, not tuned on this dataset: `dense_min_score` for dense similarities and `rerank_min_score` for reranker scores. A calibrated one was chosen on the `dev` split only. Unreranked `hybrid` has no gate: its scores are RRF values, which rank passages but aren't comparable to a cutoff.

Each calibrated cutoff is the threshold, midway between two adjacent `dev` top scores, that maximises balanced accuracy between answerable and unanswerable `dev` questions, each judged by its highest passage score, a question without passages always refused; a tie goes to the lower threshold.

- `hybrid_rerank`: 0.74 with balanced accuracy 1.000 on `dev` (49 answerable and 8 unanswerable questions) and 0.993 on `test`.
- `dense_rerank`: 0.74 with balanced accuracy 1.000 on `dev` (49 answerable and 8 unanswerable questions) and 0.993 on `test`.

A manual sample, not a measurement: 10 of 10 sampled questions were asked (every eighth answerable `test` question). They were asked with `hybrid` retrieval, reranking on, and answered by `nvidia/nemotron-3.5-super-vl:free`. Each citation was read against the section it cites by the developer during implementation, not yet checked by the owner, by this rule: a citation supports its sentence when the passage states the claim, or its own share of it when the sentence cites several passages; partly when it states only some of what it is cited for; does not support when it states none of it. Of 25 citations, 22 support the claim they are attached to, 3 partly support it and 0 don't. This review is provisional: one reader, the developer, made it during implementation, and the owner hasn't checked it. The answers were asked before eval.answers recorded each query_log id; the ids were added afterwards by matching each answer's question and text in the query log.

0 of 10 answers name a passage ("Passage 2", "the first passage") instead of citing it with a marker, counted by a text pattern. The system prompt asks the model not to, and this sample was asked with that rule in place. No run without the prompt rule exists, so this doesn't show whether the rule made the difference.

## Limits

- The questions were written by the project's author, who also built the system, so they may favour it. Gold labels name sections rather than chunks, and 33% of the answerable questions use exact terms. The owner hasn't reviewed a sample of them yet.
- The questions were written while reading the corpus's section headings, and each chunk is embedded with its heading path, so a question that echoes a heading is easier than a real user's question is likely to be.
- A gold label can't name a page's introduction, the text before its first heading: an entry with no headings covers the whole page. A question whose best answer is an introduction counts as missed when retrieval returns that introduction.
- One corpus: the English Kubernetes concepts pages. Nothing here says how retrieval does on code, OpenAPI specs or incident reports.
- Relevance is judged by section. A chunk in the right section counts even when it isn't the passage that answers the question.
- Latency is from the machine that ran the harness, not the production server.
